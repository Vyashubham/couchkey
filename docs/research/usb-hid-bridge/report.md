# Build the $20 bridge, Edge first

A Wi-Fi-driven ESP32-S3 that enumerates as a plain USB keyboard and mouse on the Xbox is **feasible, cheap (about $20–35 all-in) and worth building, but mainly for the things SmartGlass provably cannot do**: a real mouse cursor, scroll wheel and Chromium shortcuts in Edge, full keyboard navigation of the dashboard, and keyboard/mouse input in the minority of games that opt in. It is **not** a likely fix for the gap that prompted it. In Netflix and YouTube search, the one firsthand report says a physical keyboard does not type into Netflix's search grid, so expect a USB keyboard to hit the same wall SmartGlass hit there. Microsoft has said since 2018 that "most wired or wireless USB keyboards and mice will work" with no certification step, which is why a DIY HID device should enumerate where a DIY *controller* cannot. That is still an inference: nobody has publicly documented an ESP32 or RP2040 keyboard on a Series X|S, so Couchkey would be first. Buy two **Seeed XIAO ESP32S3** boards (Adafruit's QT Py ESP32-S3 is the better-documented first choice but showed out of stock on 2026-11-14), plus a short USB-A-to-USB-C *data* cable. Before the boards arrive, plug any ordinary USB keyboard and mouse into the console tonight. A bridge can only do what a real keyboard does, so that one test answers most of the per-app questions for free.

## A generic keyboard needs no handshake, and that is the whole design

Xbox treats controllers and keyboards completely differently. Controllers must pass Microsoft's licensed authentication. Since November 2023 the console has warned about, then blocked, unlicensed controller-emulating accessories such as Brook's Wingman XB 2, with error 0x82d60002 ([Pure Xbox](https://www.purexbox.com/news/2023/11/new-xbox-policy-to-block-unauthorised-accessories); [TrueAchievements](https://www.trueachievements.com/n55407/xbox-blocking-unauthorized-controllers-error-0x82d60002)). The open-source GP2040-CE firmware supports Xbox One/Series "using passthrough authentication only", which means a real controller or a licensed dongle wired inside the device ([GP2040-CE FAQ](https://gp2040-ce.info/faq/faq-general/)). Keyboards and mice have no such gate. Microsoft's 2018 announcement says **"most wired or wireless USB keyboards and mice will work"** and names no allow-list or certification ([Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/2018/09/25/mouse-and-keyboard-support-for-xbox-one-developers/)). The GDK's GameInput API treats keyboards and mice as native device classes. It reports mice as relative deltas with wheel and five buttons, and expects 125–1000 Hz keyboards with 4–6 simultaneous keys ([docs.xbox.com GameInput KB/M](https://docs.xbox.com/build/core-features/common/input/advanced/input-keyboard-mouse.md)). Composite dongle devices like Logitech's unifying combos work at least for the keyboard half. That suggests the console binds by HID usage page, not by vendor and product ID (inference, medium-high confidence).

The safest descriptor follows from this. Use a USB 2.0 full-speed composite device with one interface for a **boot-protocol 6-key-rollover keyboard** (8-byte report, no NKRO bitmap) and a second interface for a **relative mouse with wheel**. Put consumer-control media keys in a separate report or interface so a host that rejects them cannot break the keyboard. Avoid absolute or digitizer pointers. TinyPilot's absolute mouse was treated as touch on ChromeOS ([TinyPilot forum](https://forum.tinypilotkvm.com/-130/mouse-hid-gets-interpreted-as-touch-on-chromebook)), and the touchpad half of a Logitech K400 reportedly did nothing on Xbox. Microsoft has not published boot-versus-report-protocol requirements, VID/PID filtering rules or polling limits for Xbox, so the first bench test is the real proof.

## The XIAO ESP32S3 is the board to order today

The chip choice is settled. **ESP32-S2 and ESP32-S3 have a real USB 1.1 full-speed device peripheral**; the ESP32-C3 only has a fixed USB-serial/JTAG block and cannot be a HID device ([Espressif TinyUSB guide](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-iot-solution/en/latest/usb/usb_overview/tinyusb_guide.html); [Arduino-ESP32 USB API](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/arduino-esp32/en/latest/api/usb.html)). The S3 adds BLE 5 to Wi-Fi on one chip. Full-speed is plenty for keyboard and mouse at a 1 ms poll. Among S3 boards, what matters is vendor quality, antenna, connector and US stock.

The XIAO wins on practicality. It is stocked by Amazon US with fast US delivery, is cheap enough to buy in multiples, and its 8 MB PSRAM leaves room for a later mass-storage experiment. The QT Py has the stronger documentation and a proven Wi-Fi-to-HID precedent, so take it if Adafruit, DigiKey or Mouser has it. Buy **two boards**: one lives in the Xbox and receives Wi-Fi OTA updates, and the other stays on the Mac as the development unit. The Series X|S has USB-A ports ([SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1177672/how-to-use-a-keyboard-and-mouse-on-the-xbox-series-x)), so the USB-C boards need a **USB-A (male) to USB-C (male) cable explicitly sold for data/sync, 1 ft (0.3 m) ideal and 3 ft at most**. Weak, charge-only or long cables cause brownout resets during Wi-Fi transmit peaks ([Hackaday.io](https://hackaday.io/project/205906-esp32-c3-super-mini-wifi-fix-3-methods-that-work)). The Mac side needs a USB-C to USB-C data cable, which the maintainer likely owns. No specific cable listing was price-checked; expect roughly $6–10 from Anker or Amazon Basics.

Firmware on a Mac is the well-trodden path. Use **Arduino IDE 2 or PlatformIO with the Arduino-ESP32 3.x core, USB Mode set to "USB-OTG (TinyUSB)"**, and combine `USBHIDKeyboard`, `USBHIDMouse` and `USBHIDConsumerControl` under one `USB.begin()`. ESP-IDF's `esp_tinyusb` gives composite HID+MSC if needed later ([ESP-IDF USB Device Stack](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v6.0/esp32s3/api-reference/peripherals/usb_device.html)). Three documented pitfalls shape the design. Leaving CDC serial enabled alongside HID has caused SendReport errors, re-enumeration and DTR-triggered stalls ([arduino-esp32 #6922](https://github.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/issues/6922); [#9582](https://github.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/issues/9582)), so disable CDC and log over UDP. Once HID firmware runs, re-flashing over USB needs BOOT-button download mode ([Arduino forum](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/uploaded-sketch-with-hid-h-difficult-to-upload-new-sketch/1168847/8)), so build Wi-Fi OTA in from the first sketch. Keep HID buffers in internal RAM, not PSRAM.

## Steal the protocol ideas, not the GPL code

Small 2025–2026 projects already do "network in, USB HID out", and none has documented testing on an Xbox. **KMChris/esp32-kvm-ip** (MIT) is closest in shape. A Windows host sends 16-byte UDP packets to an ESP32-S3, and each packet carries the **full key and button state plus a sequence number**, so a lost packet cannot strand a key and stale packets are dropped. It sends at 125 Hz by default and found that disabling Wi-Fi modem sleep removed about 200 ms of first-packet lag ([GitHub](https://github.com/KMChris/esp32-kvm-ip)). **ferrets6/ESP32-S3-wifi-kvm** (MIT) drives an S3 from a web page over WebSocket. It releases all keys when any client disconnects and has a boot-keyboard-only build for picky hosts ([GitHub](https://github.com/ferrets6/ESP32-S3-wifi-kvm)). **touchgadget/usbkwa** runs browser-to-HID typing on the exact QT Py ESP32-S3 ([GitHub](https://github.com/touchgadget/usbkwa)). **larrylart/blue_keyboard** (MIT, 43 stars, v2.1.0) is the security reference: a T-Dongle-S3 that types passwords from phone apps over BLE, protected by bonding plus application-level mTLS ([GitHub](https://github.com/larrylart/blue_keyboard)). For BLE-first designs, **shiomachisoft/picow_ble_usb_hid_bridge** runs USB on one Pico W core and BTstack on the other, and requests a 12.5–15 ms connection interval to avoid power-saving lag ([GitHub](https://github.com/shiomachisoft/picow_ble_usb_hid_bridge)).

The most mature design is **PiKVM's Pico HID protocol**: 8-byte frames with magic byte, command and CRC16, explicit KEY down/up, RELATIVE move, BUTTON, WHEEL, a **CLEAR_HID release-all** command, and a PING whose reply carries keyboard LED and online state ([kvmd proto.py](https://github.com/pikvm/kvmd/blob/master/kvmd/plugins/hid/_mcu/proto.py)). kvmd is GPL-3.0, so re-implement the semantics rather than copying code. Commercial KVMs offer a cautionary lesson. TinyPilot's open-source build ships with **no authentication or TLS** ([TinyPilot wiki](https://github.com/tiny-pilot/tinypilot/wiki/Enabling-HTTP-Basic-Authentication-with-TinyPilot)), and WiFi Duck ships with a default "wifiduck" password and an exposed OTA endpoint ([WiFi Duck docs](https://docs.spacehuhn.com/wifiduck/howitworks/)). Couchkey should not repeat either.

## Tuned UDP lands near 10 ms; untuned Wi-Fi is ten times worse

The one rigorous benchmark measured ESP32 one-way latency at **~9 ms median for UDP, ~6 ms for TCP with Nagle off, ~14 ms for WebSocket with TCP_NODELAY, and ~5.6 ms for ESP-NOW**, but only after tuning. Default Wi-Fi configurations gave **74–84 ms medians**, and an untuned WebSocket spread from 30 to 245 ms ([Electric UI](https://electricui.com/blog/latency-comparison); [Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2024/02/11/benchmarking-latency-across-common-wireless-links-for-mcus/)). BLE stepped in roughly 7 ms increments matching the connection interval, with medians of 13–22 ms. iOS requires a minimum interval of 15 ms (11.25 ms for HID accessories) ([Apple QA1931](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/qa/qa1931/_index.html); [Silicon Labs](https://docs.silabs.com/bluetooth/3.3/bluetooth-miscellaneous-mobile/selecting-suitable-connection-parameters-for-apple-devices)). Espressif's own guidance attributes 40–200 ms UDP swings to Wi-Fi power save ([esp32.com](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=47850); [ESP-IDF Wi-Fi performance](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/latest/esp32/api-guides/wifi-driver/wifi-performance-and-power-save.html)). The USB side adds at most 1 ms at a 1 ms polling interval.

| Path (tuned) | Median one-way to MCU | Expected end-to-end on console | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Node hub → UDP → ESP32-S3 | ~1–9 ms | ~3–12 ms, tails 20–50 ms on a busy AP (estimate) | **Primary** |
| Browser → hub WebSocket → UDP | +a few ms browser/stack | ~10–25 ms (estimate) | Default UI path |
| Browser → ESP32 WebSocket directly | ~14 ms | ~15–30 ms (estimate) | Fallback if hub absent |
| Phone → BLE GATT → ESP32 | ~13–22 ms | ~15–25 ms on iOS (estimate) | Optional setup/phone fallback |
| ESP-NOW | ~5.6 ms | Needs a second ESP32 on the laptop | Not worth it |

The end-to-end figures are estimates, and nobody has measured the full chain into a console. Even so, **~10–30 ms with controlled jitter feels direct for typing and cursor work and is acceptable for casual play**. Competitive shooters are both latency-sensitive and the riskiest place to use the device anyway (see below). The recommended protocol keeps the hub as the single brain. **Browsers talk to the existing Node hub over WebSocket, and the hub speaks UDP to the board.** Each 16–24 byte packet carries magic, version, sequence number, flags, the full 8-byte boot keyboard report, accumulated mouse dx/dy as int16, wheel, buttons and a truncated HMAC. Send on a 125–250 Hz tick plus immediately on key changes, with an idle heartbeat every 50–100 ms that also keeps the radio awake. The firmware runs `WIFI_PS_NONE` and sends an all-released report after about 300 ms of silence. In-browser capture on the Mac uses Pointer Lock with `unadjustedMovement` for relative mouse deltas ([MDN Pointer Lock](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Pointer_Lock_API)). Chromium's fullscreen-only Keyboard Lock captures Esc and similar keys ([MDN Keyboard API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Keyboard_API)), but Chrome withholds Ctrl/Cmd+T/W/N from pages ([Chromium design doc](https://www.chromium.org/developers/design-documents/extensions/proposed-changes/apis-under-development/browser-keys/)). The UI therefore needs on-screen buttons for "new tab", "close tab" and "Esc" on the Xbox. A phone needs an on-screen trackpad because iOS has no pointer lock for touch.

## Hard limits, and the risks worth engineering against

Three limits are fixed. First, **a keyboard and mouse cannot become a controller**: no Guide long-press, no gameplay in titles without keyboard/mouse support, and no way around authentication short of the licensed-dongle passthrough GP2040-CE uses ([GP2040-CE FAQ](https://gp2040-ce.info/faq/faq-general/)). SmartGlass remains the gamepad path. Second, **USB cannot power the console on**. The default Shutdown (energy-saving) mode draws about 0.5 W, does not support remote wake ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/xbox-series-x-standby-mode-explained); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/news/microsoft-is-switching-your-xbox-series-x-to-energy-saving-mode-heres-what-that-means)), and is reported to cut USB power ([Technobezz](https://www.technobezz.com/xbox-series-x-controller-not-charging-fix)). Wake stays a SmartGlass job in Sleep mode, and the bridge must cold-boot and reconnect fast on every power-on. Third, **USB mass storage is a dead end for media**. The S3's 12 Mbit/s full-speed link is far too slow for video, while the Xbox Media Player already plays from DLNA servers ([How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/255016/how-to-play-video-and-music-files-on-your-xbox-one/)), so a DLNA server in the hub is the better feature.

**Ban risk is low for menus, Edge and media, and moderate only if misused in competitive games.** The Community Standards prohibit "unauthorized hardware or accessories" in general terms ([Xbox Community Standards](https://www.xbox.com/en-US/legal/community-standards)). Actual enforcement has targeted controller-auth failures and cheat devices. Activision's Ricochet now looks for "machine-driven" input patterns from devices like Cronus Zen and XIM ([Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/ricochet-anti-cheat-update-ranked-play-mod-devices/)) and pairs detection with "improved hardware banning capabilities" ([Call of Duty blog](https://www.callofduty.com/blog/2026/09/ricochet-taking-on-the-cheating-ecosystem)). Couchkey should present ordinary descriptors, never spoof Microsoft or Razer VID/PIDs (use a pid.codes ID), ship no macros or turbo, and describe itself as a UI and media remote. Avoid "Xbox" in the name and logos. Microsoft's trademark guidelines permit truthful "compatible with" statements ([Microsoft Trademark Guidelines](https://microsoft.com/legal/intellectualproperty/trademarks)).

**Security is the most serious risk.** The bridge is a network-controlled BadUSB that can browse, message and shop as the signed-in user. MITRE catalogues IP-KVM hardware as an adversary technique ([MITRE ATT&CK T1219.003](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1219/003)). Use one-time pairing that issues random per-client keys, HMAC or AEAD on every packet with replay protection, rejection of all input while unpaired, authenticated OTA only, and no fixed-password SoftAP. Add rate caps and a "disarmed on every new USB enumeration" default, which also protects a PC if the board is plugged in by mistake. Tell users to require a passkey for Xbox purchases. **Reliability** risks are stuck keys, re-enumeration across power states and updates, and brownouts from transmit peaks of up to ~500 mA ([espboards.dev](https://www.espboards.dev/troubleshooting/issues/power/esp32-brownout-reset/)). The fixes are full-state packets plus the watchdog, handling TinyUSB mount/suspend/resume without restarting Wi-Fi, reporting USB mount state to the hub, a short good cable, and a lower-TX-power option. The port's ~900 mA figure is a press estimate, not a Microsoft spec ([SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/2009446/never-plug-power-hungry-devices-xbox-usb-port)).

## Conclusion

SmartGlass failed in two distinct ways. Moving Edge's cursor is a missing input class, and USB HID fills it with officially supported behavior. Typing into Netflix and YouTube search is an app-design wall: those apps draw their own keyboards. Real keyboards probably hit that wall too, so no transport is likely to fix it. That reframes the bridge from "a better SmartGlass" to "the Edge and power-user half of Couchkey". It also makes Edge the strategic surface. Once Couchkey can drive a real browser cursor, every streaming service's web player becomes a searchable, typeable fallback.

The cheapest high-value step is not a purchase. A ten-minute test with an ordinary keyboard tonight settles the biggest uncertainty and keeps hardware time focused on what only a bridge can prove: enumeration, reconnect behavior and latency. If those three hold, Couchkey would be the first open project to publicly document a microcontroller keyboard-and-mouse bridge on Series X|S. Shipping it with pairing and authentication from day one would also set it apart from the unauthenticated KVM projects it borrows from.
